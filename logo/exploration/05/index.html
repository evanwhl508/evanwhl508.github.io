Hidden C + E

Shared Counter

Cobie and Evan share a counter, just as their names share Covan.

Same contours. Revealed.

The highlighted paths are the actual filled components used in this SVG. Faint paths show their relationship; no extra letters have been drawn over the mark.

  1. C: the open inner counter around the fold
  2. E: the inner ledge shares the outer upper and lower turns
  3. The letters are counterform readings, not separate glyphs

First impression

A rounded chamber with one inward fold and a single quiet, squared foot.

Why the shape works

The closed perimeter gives visual calm; the off-centre pocket prevents it from becoming a neutral ring. Broad strokes keep the interior useful at icon scale.

What to discover

The open negative space wraps around the inner fold like a C. The fold borrows the top and bottom of the enclosure to suggest an E-like three-level structure.

Why Covan

One letter is read in empty space and the other in the material around it: two names contribute to the same whole without two colored initials.

Could it belong to another company?

The counterform reading depends on the Cobie/Evan story. Without it, the form may be read as a rounded d; the concept offers a name-based rationale, not an untransferable symbol.

What the aqua means

The short inner fold is the shared counter’s focal point. It changes neither the outline nor the letter reading in monochrome.

Letter-reading limits

The C is negative space. The E is a shared-stroke suggestion, intentionally not a literal glyph.

Watch in review

A lowercase d or speech-form association is possible. The meaning should add interest, not be needed to rescue that first impression.

Design assessment

Subjective 1–10 judgments for discussion, not test results or a ranking. “Ownability” describes perceived visual potential; no trademark or similarity clearance is claimed. There is no total or selected winner.
