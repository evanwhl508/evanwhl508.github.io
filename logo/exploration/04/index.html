Hidden C + E

Countervoice

The first letters of two names share one curved voice.

Same contours. Revealed.

The highlighted paths are the actual filled components used in this SVG. Faint paths show their relationship; no extra letters have been drawn over the mark.

  1. C: the enclosing sweep, before the tilt
  2. E: an incomplete rhythm of shared interior levels
  3. The whole construction rotates together

First impression

A curled, oblique form with an open outer sweep and a compact returning lip.

Why the shape works

A long enclosing curve contrasts with a short internal return. Tilting the construction reduces the appearance of typeset initials.

What to discover

In the construction view, C is the enclosing stroke. An E-like rhythm appears where the return divides the interior into unequal levels; it is a suggestion, not a complete E glyph.

Why Covan

The two strokes refer to the first letters of Cobie and Evan. They borrow the same counterspace instead of sitting beside each other as CE.

Could it belong to another company?

The association is stronger for names beginning C and E, but it is still an abstract mark. The inner-return construction gives a specific naming route without proving uniqueness.

What the aqua means

Aqua sits on the returning voice as it enters the shared enclosure. It identifies the fusion rather than spelling a colored E.

Letter-reading limits

C is structural; E is an incomplete counterform reading. No literal CE is overlaid.

Watch in review

The letter reading is less immediate than the curl. At small sizes, judge the large counter and opening rather than the E suggestion.

Design assessment

Subjective 1–10 judgments for discussion, not test results or a ranking. “Ownability” describes perceived visual potential; no trademark or similarity clearance is claimed. There is no total or selected winner.
